Meet the case.

An employer confirmed the candidate’s dates and role, but the completed record carries an older title. The dates agree. The wording does not. A traditional workflow sends the completed search to a reviewer, who reconstructs what happened and decides whether the difference matters.

The AI QA worker begins with the same question a strong reviewer would ask: What does the complete record support?

01

The worker completes the case before asking the AI.

The model receives one bounded record, not an invitation to explore the operation.

What happens

The system collects the current result, supplied and verified facts, supporting evidence, lifecycle state, and the rules that apply to this product. Information that does not belong to the decision stays out.

What the case gains
  • One current version of every decision fact
  • The exact handbook rules in scope
  • The actions this case is allowed to take
  • No historical reviewer answer to copy
02

The AI investigates the difference instead of guessing.

It may ask for evidence. It may not roam.

What happens

The AI notices that the titles differ while the employer and dates agree. It checks the evidence already attached. If a decision fact is still missing, it can request an approved read-only lookup for this case alone. The answer returns with a receipt.

What the case gains
  • A clear account of what matches
  • The exact difference that matters
  • Receipts for every retrieved fact
  • No evidence borrowed from another case
03

The AI must commit to a business decision.

There is no free-form instruction and no hidden third outcome.

What happens

The handbook says the final record must reflect the supported verified title. The AI therefore chooses change. It identifies one field, the value it expects to find now, the replacement, the evidence, and the QA action that follows.

What the case gains
  • One decision: approve or change
  • An exact before-and-after value
  • The handbook basis
  • The evidence supporting the change
The proposed decision

Change the final title, then complete QA.

The AI explains that the employer-supported title is more specific and belongs in the completed record.

expected current value: Operations Analyst
replacement: Business Operations Analyst
evidence: employer verification receipt
next action: complete QA
04

The harness tries to disprove the action.

Good reasoning is not permission to write.

What happens

Plain code checks that the cited evidence belongs to this case, the rule exists, the field may be changed, the old value still matches, the replacement is supported, and the requested QA action is allowed.

What the harness proves
  • The evidence is real and case-bound
  • The source has not changed underneath the AI
  • The requested field and action are allowed
  • The proposed value follows from the evidence
If any check fails

The machine writes nothing. The case stays exactly as it was, the failed condition is recorded, and unrelated work continues. New evidence or a repaired connection can wake the case later. Uncertainty does not become silent approval or a fallback QA queue.

05

The machine acts once—and then proves what it did.

Execution is not complete until the destination agrees.

What happens

The machine applies the verified title change and the required QA action. It immediately reads the destination back and compares the actual values with the approved decision. A duplicate instruction cannot apply the change twice.

What leaves the worker
  • The confirmed final title
  • The completed QA action
  • The before-and-after values
  • A receipt-backed audit history

The result is more than a verdict.

The case finishes with a decision, the evidence behind it, the exact action taken, and readback proving the destination now agrees. That record becomes evidence for monitoring and later improvement, but it does not rewrite the live worker on the spot.

The AI handles the judgment that ordinary rules cannot. The harness turns that judgment into a safe, bounded, provable action.
