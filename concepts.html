A machine that knows when it doesn't know

Follow one verification through five rooms. Watch its identity disappear, its evidence take shape, the model draft an answer, and the system decide whether that answer deserves to leave the building.

QA repair harness team  ·  companion to the implementation deep dive

Every automated decision system reveals itself when it is unsure. Some guess. Some hide uncertainty behind polished language. This one stops, records why it stopped, and hands the case to a person.

THE TRAVELING CASEThe provided employment dates and the verified dates differ. The record may need a return — or the difference may be harmless. And one piece of evidence is missing.

The problem

Two ways to be wrong, one of them expensive

A reviewer can be wrong in two directions. Send a correct record back and it takes a measured detour — roughly eight days before approval returns. Approve an incorrect record and the error ships to a client inside a background check. In the first half of 2026, about 2% of finished verifications returned with a coded QA error — a floor, not a census — and most were procedural and rule-shaped.

A language model raises the stakes because its wrong answer can be confident, fluent, and perfectly formatted. The architecture therefore cannot be "ask the model and trust it when it sounds sure."

The idea

Put the model in the smallest room

Most systems that put an LLM to work share a familiar shape: the model sits in the middle, the decision flows through it, and safety is a perimeter — filters and guardrails wrapped around a trusted core. This harness inverts that. The decision-maker is a stack of plain, predictable software — gates, precedent lookups, a verifier that re-checks everything — and the model is a small, sealed, swappable box off to one side that produces drafts.

The codebase gives the game away in its vocabulary: the model component is called the reasoner, never the decider. And the very first sentence the model reads, on every single case, is this:

"You are the replaceable reasoning component in a rule-governed QA repair harness."

— the production system prompt, opening line

The tour

Follow one case through the building

Five rooms, one case, and a fast lane for questions people have already settled.

ROOM 1Keep the facts. Lose the identity.

THE CASE · ROOM 1Loses its identity, keeps its shape. The name is gone, the note is now “note present,” the id is a hash — what remains: two date ranges that differ, and one evidence flag missing.

Room 1 turns the warehouse row into a fact packet: the case keeps its logical shape and loses its identity.

The same privacy screen runs before every hosted request and after every model response. The hosted connection refuses any payload richer than the stripped tier. Privacy is checked by the system, not promised by the team.

ROOM 2Let the cheap questions go first.

THE CASE · ROOM 2Gains its rule scope — and a chalk mark: “evidence gap.” No confirmed precedent matches these exact values and the context is incomplete, so the fast lane is closed. The case walks on.

Plain code asks the cheap questions first: is the case in scope, and does the required evidence exist? A hard failure goes directly to a person. A complete match to a human-confirmed precedent takes the fast lane through code. Missing context closes the shortcut and sends the case onward.

ROOM 3Give the model a form, not a microphone.

THE CASE · ROOM 3Gains a draft. The model proposes “needs a fix,” citing the date rule and the mismatch it can see.

Only now does the model enter the story, and it works the way a contractor works in a secure facility: in a sealed room, with only the documents handed through the slot, filling out a form.

The model must return one of three answers: fix it, accept it, or send it to a person. Decoding enforces the form, and every claim must cite the allowed rules and evidence. A better model can later occupy the same slot without changing the surrounding controls.

The model can request one of seven read-only lookups: timeline, evidence, code meanings, safe comparisons, rules, and product context. Every answer returns with a receipt the verifier can inspect. Repeat a question, request something unavailable, or exhaust the budget, and the case goes to a person. Whatever happens, the model still leaves the room with the same locked form.

ROOM 4Never grade your own homework.

THE CASE · ROOM 4Gains six stamps — five green, one red. The proposed fix leans on evidence the packet doesn't contain: the chalk mark from room 2 just became a hard refusal.

The draft now meets the verifier, and the verifier's stance is the whole system in miniature: it doesn't ask whether the draft sounds right; it re-derives every checkable claim from the same inputs. Six stamps, all of which must land:

✓

Citations exist. Every id the model cited exists on the allowed list it was handed. An invented citation isn't a deduction — it's an automatic block.

✓

Claims match evidence. A "fix it" or "it's fine" must rest on at least one rule and one piece of evidence — and the cited rule must actually govern the outcome claimed.

✓

Confidence clears the floor. Below the 65% confidence threshold, the model may not commit either way. It can only recommend a human.

✓

Nothing sensitive appears. The same privacy screen from room 1 runs over the model's own output — in case the model hallucinated something that looks like personal data.

✓

Repair claims have proof. "This was already corrected" requires field-level before/after proof upstream. Without it, the claim cannot stand.

✓

Code meanings are known. Status codes with no authoritative mapping may be echoed, never interpreted. The model doesn't get to guess what code 7 means.

ROOM 5Three doors, with the floor tilted toward a person.

THE CASE · ROOM 5Gains its route: rewritten to human review, proposed codes stripped, the reason attached. A person decides, with the whole trail in hand.

The green door opens only when every check passes. Human review is the default. A blocked draft is rewritten before routing: its verdict becomes human review, its actionable codes are removed, and the reason travels with the case. No route produces a silent approval.

The memory

Every confirmed answer is one less question for the model

When a human confirms a ruling, it becomes precedent. The next matching case takes the fast lane through code, and the model's jurisdiction shrinks by one case pattern. Instead of trying to make the model trusted with more, the system moves every hard-won certainty into code — free, immediate, and auditable.

The proof

The final test stays sealed

The model does not get to practice on the final exam. The evaluation is built with the same suspicion as the pipeline:

The staircase starts with canaries — synthetic cases with known answers that run before every batch; a single miss quarantines the batch. It ends at a blind set the team has deliberately never scored against, reserved for the day a candidate earns it. In between sits the replay benchmark, whose honesty features are worth naming: the model predicts from exactly the information the original human reviewer had (everything they wrote afterward is withheld), the labels are treated as "what the human did," not "the truth," and every score is compared against a deliberately embarrassing baseline — a three-line fixed rule.

That tie is the most credible thing about the project. A lesser evaluation setup would have produced a happier number — and early, smaller runs did, until scale washed the mirage out. The harness's own instruments caught it: gates held, every unsupported commitment was blocked rather than shipped, and the disagreements between model and rule were harvested as new precedent candidates for the ledger. A benchmark you can't fail is marketing. This one can say "not yet," and it has.

In one sentence

The shape of the idea

Strip identity at the door. Settle cheap questions in code. Let the model investigate and draft on a locked form. Rebuild every claim. Route uncertainty to a person. Turn confirmed rulings into precedent.
