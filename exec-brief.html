Executive briefing

We built the worker that can turn the final manual QA decision in Verifications into machine work.

The AI reads the complete case, investigates missing facts, and returns one exact decision: approve it or make a supported change. Independent checks verify the evidence and limit what may change. The machine then acts once and confirms the result.

Most of the background check is automated. Its final decision is not.

Technology already handles much of intake, research, outreach, and routine fulfillment. Standard work moves faster, but nearly every completed education or employment verification still arrives at the same final human pass.

QA touched 95% of the education and employment searches measured in the first half of 2026. A reviewer reconstructed the case, compared what was provided with what was verified, applied the handbook, and decided whether the result could leave the operation.

When QA found a mistake, the reviewer usually reacted quickly. The delay came afterward: corrections crossed queues and returned for another review. A caught mistake could add roughly eight elapsed days before approval returned—not eight days of hands-on work, but eight days in the case journey.

The missing capability was not another checklist. It was a worker that could understand the whole case, make the QA decision, carry out the exact supported change, and record what happened.

That is a floor, not a ceiling. The remaining cases define the next build: richer case packages and approved lookups—not a permanent review queue.

The AI makes the judgment. Independent checks control what happens next.

This is not a classifier glancing at one row. The AI receives the complete case and the same handbook the team uses. When a decision fact is missing, it may request an approved read-only lookup tied to that case. Every lookup leaves a receipt.

The AI returns one of two business decisions: approve the case as it stands, or make exact supported changes. A change identifies the field, its current value, the replacement, the supporting evidence, and the QA action that follows. The model cannot issue an open-ended command.

The record shows. The AI investigates and decides. Independent checks verify the action. The machine acts once. Readback confirms. Outcomes improve later releases.

The AI handles judgment

Investigates dates that nearly match, titles that may mean the same thing, incomplete evidence, and several rules interacting at once.

Explains the decision through case evidence and the handbook.

Proposes only an approval or exact changes.

The harness controls consequences

Rejects invented citations, evidence from another case, unsupported changes, stale facts, and fields the worker may not change.

Limits every write to an approved action.

Confirms the case matches the decision before the work is finished.

When independent checks cannot verify the action, nothing changes.

A supported decision travels through a controlled route. The machine applies it once, reads the case back immediately, and compares the actual result with the approved change. No person approves the individual AI decision.

If evidence is missing or a connection fails, the case stays unchanged while unrelated work continues. New evidence or a repaired connection can wake it automatically. A serious integrity failure stops later writes until targeted checks confirm the problem is gone.

Four findings shaped the worker we built.

Finding 01

The final check was the remaining constraint.

QA touched nearly every measured search. As routine fulfillment automated, the final quality decision became a larger share of the work still attached to each case.

Finding 02

The delay lived after the review.

Rework usually began within an hour, but approval returned roughly eight elapsed days later. Removing the round trip matters more than asking someone to work it faster.

Finding 03

Most coded mistakes looked like rules.

In the first half of 2026, about 2% of finished education and employment verifications received a coded QA return. About 86% of those coded reasons fell into four procedural categories.

Finding 04

Better context mattered more than another prompt.

Testing old cases showed that better protected context helped the AI more than rewriting its instructions. That is why the worker receives the complete case and may retrieve only approved missing facts.

Testing covered the failures that matter in live work: a case changing mid-decision, the same instruction arriving twice, a write failing halfway, or readback being unable to confirm what changed. In each scenario, the worker either confirmed the intended result or left the case unchanged and stopped later writes.

The case moves immediately. The system improves deliberately.

In operation, every completed case will create two products: a decision that moves the case now, and evidence that can improve later decisions. Those products move on different clocks.

In operation, a separate monitor will watch performance across many cases—especially approvals, changes, unresolved cases, and unusual shifts over time. It will be able to stop writes when a severe pattern appears, but it will not alter or route an individual case. New rules, approved actions, prompts, and models will be tested offline before they change production behavior.

The destination is an autonomous QA worker—not another review queue.

In production, the worker will take only the case types it has been built and tested to handle. Within that scope, it will approve the case or make the exact supported change automatically. Cases outside that scope stay in their ordinary workflow. A case it cannot verify remains unchanged until the missing dependency is repaired. Neither creates a fallback QA queue.

People maintain the handbook, approved actions, and connections to other systems. They watch performance across many cases and repair the worker when something breaks. They do not approve individual AI decisions or become the finalizer for machine writes.

With the worker active, the background check ends with a decision the AI can explain, an action independent checks can verify, and a result the operation can learn from.

Evidence boundary

The 60% floor covers finalized education and employment cases that genuinely entered QA in the first half of 2026. It estimates phase-one addressability from evidence already in the record—not live automation, autonomous accuracy, labor savings, or staffing reduction. The worker has been tested for decision behavior, controlled execution, and a complete audit history. Live operation is still required to establish production accuracy and operational impact.
