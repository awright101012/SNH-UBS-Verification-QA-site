Executive brief·July 2026·7 min

The last manual step in Verifications is QA. I built an AI agent to automate it.

Nearly every finished education and employment verification still ends with a person reconstructing the case, applying the handbook, and deciding whether it is ready to ship. I built an AI agent to move that same pass earlier. It investigates the record, reasons from the same handbook, and shows the evidence behind its answer. The harness verifies every claim; reviewers keep every uncertain case.

The destination  universal human review to AI-led QA  ·  The measured cost  a caught mistake can still add roughly eight days  ·  The next gate  reviewer feedback

The operating problem

Automation can finish the search. It cannot certify its own answer.

Technology is steadily taking repetitive work out of intake, research, outreach, and fulfillment. That is real progress: routine work moves faster and people can concentrate on exceptions.

But automation still hands nearly every finished search to QA. The final human check touches 95% of searches. A reviewer reconstructs the case, compares what was provided with what was verified, applies the handbook, and decides whether the answer is ready to ship.

When QA finds a mistake, reviewers react quickly. The delay comes afterward, as the record crosses queues, corrections, and another review. A caught mistake starts a multi-day return loop.

Most coded return reasons fall into a handful of procedural categories: routing, discrepancy classification, incomplete fields, and note handling. These are repeatable, rule-shaped mistakes being caught after the work is complete.

The shared problem is timing and memory. The check happens after completion, and each ruling closes one case instead of becoming a reusable answer for the next one.

The opportunity is not to make reviewers read faster. It is to move the check earlier and let the operation remember what reviewers have already settled.

The response

What we built: an AI reviewer that can investigate

The agent runs the same narrow pass a reviewer runs on completed education and employment verifications. It must choose one of three answers: the record is correct, it needs a fix, or a person needs to look.

This is not a classifier glancing at one row. The AI can investigate. It can pull the timeline, inspect the evidence, compare provided and verified facts, look up code meanings, consult the handbook, and explain why its answer follows. Every lookup leaves a receipt.

The handbook has been turned into rules the system can apply and cite. A citation to a rule that does not exist is an automatic stop. Historical QA decisions became the benchmark and the beginning of a precedent ledger, so the agent is tested against how the operation actually handled real cases — not against invented examples.

The record shows. The AI investigates. The harness verifies and routes. Reviewers decide and apply. Confirmed rulings teach the system.

The control handoff

The harness makes AI reasoning operationally trustworthy

The AI handles the part that resists ordinary automation: dates that almost match, titles that may mean the same thing, incomplete evidence, and several handbook rules interacting at once. It can ask questions, connect facts across the record, and explain the judgment it reached.

The harness handles what must never vary. It knows which evidence is allowed, whether a citation is real, whether a proposed code exists, whether required facts are missing, and which routes are permitted. Before the model sees a case, names, free-text notes, and identifiers are stripped, hashed, or reduced to safe flags. After the model answers, the harness independently rebuilds every claim.

The AI is not reduced to a text generator. It is given room to investigate and reason. The harness gives that reasoning a safe path into the operation. A model alone can produce a persuasive answer; this combination produces an answer that can survive inspection.

The system recognizes ten routes, and seven are refusals. Only a fully supported answer can pass. When a check fails, the proposed action is removed and the case is rewritten for human review. The AI can be wrong. It cannot be silently wrong.

WHAT THE HARNESS PROVED

Six independent checks ran on every benchmark case, and zero unsupported commitments escaped them. Every answer must earn its route.

The proof

Three findings changed the case for AI-led QA

The delay lives after the review. Rework usually begins within an hour of a return, but approval comes back roughly eight days later. Moving the check earlier attacks the round trip, not the reviewer.

WHAT THE DATA MEASURES

The eight days are elapsed time, not hands-on effort. The evidence proves the calendar cost of the return loop; it does not yet support a labor-savings claim. Reviewer activity logs will measure that separately.

Most coded mistakes look like rules. About 2% of finished verifications returned with a coded error in the first half of 2026 — a floor, not a census — and about 86% of coded return reasons fell into four procedural categories. That makes earlier, repeatable checking a credible target.

Better evidence mattered more than another clever prompt. The benchmark asks whether the AI can reproduce the action a reviewer took better than a deliberately simple fixed rule. The full benchmark ended in a statistical tie. When the agent received richer but still anonymous case facts, it produced a small early lead.

The result points to context, not prompting, as the next source of improvement. A reviewer also reasons from the whole case — not from a single row.

WHAT EXISTS NOW

The codified handbook, investigative tools, privacy boundary, locked answer form, independent verifier, audit trail, precedent ledger, replay benchmark, and sealed blind test are built. This is no longer an idea waiting for an architecture. It is an agent entering reviewer feedback.

The learning operation

Every hard call becomes an answer the system can remember

Most AI systems improve by asking the model to do more. This one improves by turning settled judgment into reusable precedent.

The latest benchmark surfaced precedent candidates from roughly 3% of its cases. Reviewer confirmation turns those candidates into durable memory for the operation.

The path forward

The destination is AI-led QA. Reviewer evidence earns it.

Built and testedNextEarned future
Codified handbook, investigative tools, privacy boundary, locked answer, independent verifier, audit trail, replay benchmark, and sealed blind test. Reviewer-controlled cases, corrections and overrides, confirmed precedents, and complete outcome measurement. The agent checks every case; people review by risk; senior reviewers own disputes, calibration, and new precedent.

This is how universal manual QA is replaced: not by discarding human judgment, but by reserving it for the cases that genuinely need it. Routine checks become continuous machine work. Senior reviewers own disputes, new precedent, and calibration. The operation gains both broader coverage and better memory.

The decision now is whether to put the agent beside the QA team, keep every action under reviewer control, and measure which universal checks it can safely absorb.

That is the bridge from universal manual review to AI-led QA — and the point where QA stops being where automation ends.
