Executive briefing

We built the worker that removes the last manual step in Verifications.

The AI QA worker reads the complete case, investigates what is missing, applies the same handbook as the team, and decides: approve or make an exact change. The harness proves the decision before the machine can act, then readback confirms what happened.

Most of the background check is automated. Its final decision is not.

Technology already handles much of intake, research, outreach, and routine fulfillment. Standard work moves faster, but nearly every completed education or employment verification still arrives at the same final human pass.

QA touched 95% of the education and employment searches measured in the first half of 2026. A reviewer reconstructed the case, compared what was provided with what was verified, applied the handbook, and decided whether the result could leave the operation.

When QA found a mistake, the reviewer usually reacted quickly. The delay came afterward: corrections crossed queues and returned for another review. A caught mistake could add roughly eight elapsed days before approval returned—not eight days of hands-on work, but eight days in the case journey.

The missing capability was not another checklist. It was a worker that could understand the whole case, make the QA decision, carry it through safely, and preserve proof of what happened.

The AI makes the judgment. The harness makes it safe to act.

This is not a classifier glancing at one row. The AI receives the complete case and the same handbook the team uses. When a decision fact is missing, it may request an approved read-only lookup tied to that case. Every lookup leaves a receipt.

The AI returns one of two business decisions: approve the case as it stands, or make exact supported changes. A change identifies the field, its current value, the replacement, the supporting evidence, and the QA action that follows. The model cannot issue an open-ended command.

The case shows. The AI decides. The harness proves. The machine acts. Readback confirms. Outcomes teach.

The AI handles judgment

Investigates dates that nearly match, titles that may mean the same thing, incomplete evidence, and several rules interacting at once.

Explains the decision through case evidence and the handbook.

Proposes only an approval or exact changes.

The harness controls consequences

Rejects invented citations, evidence from another case, unsupported changes, stale facts, and unauthorized fields.

Limits every write to an approved action.

Confirms the destination matches the decision before the case is complete.

When the system cannot prove a safe action, nothing changes.

A supported decision travels through a narrow write path. The machine applies it once, reads the destination back immediately, and compares the actual result with the approved change. No person approves the individual AI decision.

If evidence is missing or a connection fails, the case stays unchanged while unrelated work continues. New evidence or a repaired connection can wake it automatically. A serious integrity failure stops later writes until the system can prove the problem is gone.

Four findings shaped the worker we built.

Finding 01

The final check was the remaining constraint.

QA touched nearly every measured search. As routine fulfillment automated, the final quality decision became a larger share of the work still attached to each case.

Finding 02

The delay lived after the review.

Rework usually began within an hour, but approval returned roughly eight elapsed days later. Removing the round trip matters more than asking someone to work it faster.

Finding 03

Most coded mistakes looked like rules.

In the first half of 2026, about 2% of finished education and employment verifications received a coded QA return. About 86% of those coded reasons fell into four procedural categories.

Finding 04

Better context mattered more than another prompt.

Testing old cases showed that better protected context helped the AI more than rewriting its instructions. That is why the worker receives the complete case and may retrieve only approved missing facts.

Testing covered the failures that matter in live work: a case changing mid-decision, the same instruction arriving twice, a write failing halfway, or the system being unable to prove what changed. In each scenario, the worker either confirmed the intended result or left the case unchanged and stopped later writes.

The case moves immediately. The system improves deliberately.

Every completed case creates two products: a decision that moves the case now, and evidence that can improve later decisions. Those products move on different clocks.

A separate monitor watches performance across many cases—especially approvals, changes, unresolved cases, and unusual shifts over time. It can stop writes when a severe pattern appears, but it cannot alter or route an individual case. New rules, approved actions, prompts, and models are tested offline before they replace what is live.

QA becomes an autonomous worker—not another queue.

The worker takes only the case types it has been built and tested to handle. It completes those cases automatically: approve, or make an exact change and take the required QA action. Cases outside that scope stay in their ordinary workflow. A case it cannot safely finish remains unchanged until the missing dependency is repaired. Neither creates a fallback QA queue.

People maintain the handbook, approved actions, and connections to other systems. They watch performance across many cases and repair the worker when something breaks. They do not approve individual AI decisions or become the finalizer for machine writes.

With the worker active, the background check ends with a decision the AI can explain, an action the harness can prove, and a result the operation can learn from.

Evidence boundary

The operating measurements describe education and employment verifications from the first half of 2026 and the prior human QA loop. The repository-owned worker has been built and tested for behavior, containment, and auditability. Live operation is still required to establish production accuracy and operational impact.
